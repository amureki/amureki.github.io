I always loved the position of Caps Lock and hated what it did. Why give such an easy-to-reach key a function I almost never use?

Several years ago, I discovered the idea of a Hyper key. On a Mac, that means pressing Control, Option, Command, and Shift together. You map all four modifiers to one key, and suddenly you have a whole set of shortcuts that apps and the OS usually leave alone.

So I replaced Caps Lock with Hyper. Now I hold it with my left pinky and press another key to do whatever I assigned to that combination. Finally, some work for that pinky!

It felt like magic. It still does.

## Everything gets a shortcut

Most of my Hyper shortcuts take me to an app:

- `Hyper+B`: browser
- `Hyper+T`: terminal
- `Hyper+S`: Slack
- `Hyper+C`: coding environment, T3 Code
- `Hyper+E`: editor
- `Hyper+N`: notes

And `Hyper+Space` switches input languages. I do that often enough to have [written about it before](/notes/2025-03-07-fast-input-switch-macos/).

Any app I use on my laptop more than once a day deserves a Hyper shortcut. The letters mostly match what I use the app for, so I don't have much to memorize.

## I already know where I want to go

I used to hate opening the app switcher with `Cmd+Tab` or looking at the Dock. I knew which app I wanted. But there I was, looking through icons to find it, then getting distracted by notification badges and other shiny things.

I only wanted to open the browser. Why am I looking at Slack?

Now I press `Hyper+B`, and I am in the browser. `Hyper+T`, and I am in the terminal. I don't use the app switcher or the Dock anymore.

If an app doesn't have a shortcut, I open Raycast and type the first couple of letters of its name.

Caps Lock was always in the right place. I finally gave it something useful to do.
